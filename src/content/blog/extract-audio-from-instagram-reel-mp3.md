To extract the audio from an Instagram Reel, copy the Reel's link, paste it into the [Instagram Reel audio downloader](/audio), and tap **Download MP3**. You get the full soundtrack of the Reel — the song, the voice-over, or the original sound — as a regular MP3 file you can keep, play anywhere, or set as a ringtone.

## "Save audio" in Instagram is not a download

Tap the audio name at the bottom of any Reel and Instagram opens that sound's page with a **Save audio** button. It is easy to assume that saves the song. It does not.

| | Instagram "Save audio" | Extract as MP3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it ends up | A *Saved* list inside Instagram | A file in your Downloads folder |
| Works offline | No | Yes |
| Use in your own Reel | Yes, from the audio picker | Yes, as part of a video you upload |
| Use as ringtone or alarm | No | Yes |
| Play in other apps | No | Yes |

So if you want the sound for a new Reel made inside Instagram, *Save audio* is enough. For everything else you need the file.

## Extract audio from a Reel in three steps

1. **Copy the link.** On the Reel, tap the **three dots** or the **share arrow**, then **Copy link**.
2. **Paste it.** Open the [audio downloader](/audio), paste the link into the box, and press Enter.
3. **Save the MP3.** After a few seconds, tap **Download MP3**.

You can also start from the **audio page** itself. Tap the song name on the Reel, tap the three dots on the audio page, and choose **Copy link**. That link works in the same box.

The steps are identical on iPhone, Android, Windows, and Mac. Nothing is installed and you do not log in to Instagram.

### Where the MP3 is saved

- **iPhone:** *Files → Downloads*. It will not appear in Apple Music; play it from Files or share it to another app.
- **Android:** the *Download* folder. Music players such as YouTube Music, Samsung Music, or VLC pick it up from there.
- **Computer:** your browser's Downloads folder.

## Use the Reel audio as a ringtone

### On Android

1. Open **Settings → Sound & vibration → Phone ringtone**.
2. Tap **My Sounds** (Pixel) or the **+** button (Samsung).
3. Pick the MP3 from your Download folder.

Android accepts the whole file, though a ringtone only plays for about 30 seconds before the call goes to voicemail.

### On iPhone

iOS 26 made this simple. The clip has to be **shorter than 30 seconds**, so trim it first if needed.

1. Open the **Files** app and find the MP3 in *Downloads*.
2. Tap and hold the file, then tap **Share**.
3. Choose **Use as Ringtone**. If you do not see it, tap *More*.

The tone appears at the top of the list in *Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone*.

On older versions of iOS there is no direct option. The free **GarageBand** app can import the MP3 and export it as a ringtone, which takes a few more minutes.

## Use it as an alarm

- **Android:** open the **Clock** app, tap an alarm, tap the sound name, then **Add new** and choose the MP3.
- **iPhone:** alarms use the ringtone list. Add the clip as a ringtone using the steps above, then go to *Clock → Alarms → Edit → Sound* and select it.

## Trim the clip first

Reels often start with a few seconds you do not want. Two easy ways to cut the MP3:

- **On a phone:** many file managers and music apps include a trim tool, and Samsung's built-in sound picker lets you choose the highlight.
- **On a computer:** <a href="https://www.audacityteam.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Audacity</a> is free. Open the MP3, select the part you want, and export the selection.

## When there is no audio to extract

- **The audio was removed.** If the Reel shows "This audio is no longer available", the track is gone at the source.
- **The Reel is private.** Private posts cannot be reached by link.
- **The post is a photo.** Photo posts with a song attached do not always carry an audio file that can be extracted.
- **The link is wrong.** A profile link or a search page is not a Reel link. Copy it again from the Reel itself.

If you actually want the video with its sound and not only the sound, see [how to save Reels in your gallery with music](/blog/save-instagram-reels-in-gallery-with-music).

## Using Reel audio responsibly

Most songs you hear in Reels are commercial recordings. Keeping a clip on your own phone as a ringtone is personal use. Putting that song into a video you publish, or sharing the file, is a different matter and normally needs permission from whoever owns the recording. If you need music for your own content, pick a track from Instagram's audio library inside the app or use royalty-free music.
