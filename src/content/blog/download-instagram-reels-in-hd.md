To download an Instagram Reel in HD, save it by link instead of screen recording it: paste the Reel's link into the [Instagram video downloader](/) and it saves the largest MP4 Instagram serves for that post, up to **1080 × 1920**, with the original sound. If the file still looks soft, the blur is in Instagram's own copy, and no tool can take it out.

## What HD means on Instagram

Instagram re-compresses every upload and keeps the result at a maximum size for each format. When you watch, the app streams whichever version suits your connection, which is why a Reel can look sharp on Wi-Fi and blocky on mobile data. A downloader can skip that and ask for the largest version Instagram has.

| Format | Shape | Largest size Instagram keeps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reel | 9:16 vertical | 1080 × 1920 (Full HD) |
| Story | 9:16 vertical | 1080 × 1920 |
| Feed video | Square, portrait, or landscape | 1080 pixels wide |
| Photo | Square, portrait, or landscape | 1080 pixels wide |

So "HD" on Instagram means 1080p at most, and 720p for some videos. There is no 4K version to download, even if the creator filmed in 4K.

## Why a downloaded Reel can look blurry

**It was uploaded that way.** A Reel exported at 720p, filmed in low light, or re-uploaded from TikTok or a WhatsApp forward has already lost detail before Instagram compresses it again.

**The creator's app uploaded a lighter version.** On a slow connection, Instagram can upload a smaller version of a video unless the creator turned on *Upload at highest quality*.

**It is an older post.** Videos posted years ago were often stored at 640 or 720 pixels wide. That is the largest copy Instagram has, so it is the largest any downloader can give you.

**It is a screen recording.** Recording your screen captures it at screen resolution, re-encodes it, and includes the app interface. It is always softer than the file itself.

**It was re-compressed when shared.** Messaging apps shrink videos to send them faster. A Reel that looks sharp on your phone can arrive blurry on a friend's.

**It is shown larger than it is.** A 1080 × 1920 video looks crisp on a phone, but stretched to full screen on a large monitor it looks softer. That is the size of the file, not a fault in the download.

## How to get the sharpest copy

1. **Use the original post.** Download from the creator's own Reel, not from a repost or a compilation account. Every re-upload is compressed again.
2. **Download by link, not by screen recording.** Copy the link, paste it into [ReelsDl.net](/), and tap **Download video**. You get the MP4 Instagram streams, with no interface on top and no watermark.
3. **Check the resolution.** On iPhone, open the video in Photos and swipe up. On Android, open it in Gallery and tap ⋮ → **Details**. On Windows, right-click → **Properties** → **Details**; on a Mac, **Get Info**. A Reel at 1080 × 1920 is Full HD.
4. **Share it without re-compression.** In WhatsApp, choose the HD option before sending, or send the video as a document. AirDrop, Google Drive, and email attachments keep the file as it is.

The same applies to photos: the [photo downloader](/photo) saves each image at the size Instagram stores, which is sharper than a screenshot on most phones.

## Do "HD", "1080p", or "4K" downloaders give better quality?

Every honest downloader gets the same file from Instagram, so none can be sharper than the original. A site that promises 4K Instagram downloads is upscaling: it stretches the same pixels into a larger frame. AI upscalers can make edges look cleaner, but they invent detail rather than recover it, and the file gets several times bigger.

What a downloader can get wrong is picking a smaller version than the best one available, or re-encoding the video on its own servers. ReelsDl.net picks the largest MP4 Instagram serves for each post and streams it to you unchanged.

## For creators: upload Reels in the best quality

If your own Reels look worse on Instagram than on your phone:

- In Instagram, go to **Settings and activity → Data usage and media quality** and turn on **Upload at highest quality**.
- Export at **1080 × 1920**, 9:16, at 30 frames per second. Larger files are scaled down anyway.
- Upload the original file, not a copy that has already been through WhatsApp or another app.
- Upload over Wi-Fi so the app does not fall back to a lighter version.
